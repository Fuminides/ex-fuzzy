Ordinal “at least / at most” rules#

A condition of a rule is by default one linguistic term: IF no2 IS Fair. When the terms of a variable are ordered (Low, Medium, High, …) a rule often means a range: “Fair or worse”, “Medium or better”. ordinal_conditions=True lets the genetic search write such conditions:

IF no2_last IS at least Fair AND humidity IS at most Medium WITH DS 0.41, ACC 0.83

  • x IS at least t is the union of t and every term above it (up to the highest term).

  • x IS at most t is the union of every term below t (from the lowest term) and t.

The terms of a variable are ordered as they are listed in its partition, from low to high. The partitions the library builds (utils.construct_partitions) are listed in that order; a partition you give is checked and a UserWarning says when its terms are not listed from low to high.

Two further options select where the conditions apply and add named compound terms (see below): ordinal_conditions also takes a collection of features, so that a cyclic variable such as a clock hour does not get at most Medium, and compound_terms adds named unions of terms that need not be contiguous.

The option is off by default. Off, nothing changes: the gene encoding, the rules, the printed text, the saved files and every seeded fit are those of 3.4.0 (a test replays nine fits recorded on that release).

Usage#

from sklearn.datasets import load_iris
from ex_fuzzy import BaseFuzzyRulesClassifier

X, y = load_iris(return_X_y=True)
model = BaseFuzzyRulesClassifier(nRules=15, nAnts=2, n_linguistic_variables=4, ordinal_conditions=True,
                                 n_gen=30, pop_size=50, random_state=0, patience=None)
model.fit(X, y)
model.print_rules()
# Rules for consequent: 2
# IF 0 IS at least Medium AND 3 IS at least High WITH DS 0.19, ACC 0.96

# The operator of every condition, without parsing the text:
for rule in model.rule_base.get_rules():
    for condition in rule.conditions(model.rule_base.antecedents):
        print(condition.variable, condition.operator, condition.term, condition.terms)
# ...
# 3 at_most Medium ['Low', 'Medium']
# 0 at_least Medium ['Medium', 'High', 'Very High']
# 3 at_least High ['High', 'Very High']

The option exists on the three estimators the search runs through: BaseFuzzyRulesClassifier (winning-rule and mixture inference, also with mixture_search), BaseFuzzyRulesRegressor (crisp and fuzzy consequents) and the problems behind them, FitRuleBase and FitRuleBaseRegression. It works with searched partitions and with linguistic_variables you give (the given variables are not modified: the search uses copies), and with Type-1 and interval Type-2 sets. It is refused for general Type-2 sets and together with candidate_rules (which only chooses among given rules). Categorical variables keep single terms, since their categories are not ordered; so do variables with fewer than 3 terms.

Per-variable selection#

ordinal_conditions accepts True (every variable that can have them), False (the default) or a collection of features, by name or by index (a list, tuple, set or array; names are the variable names, or the column names of a DataFrame):

BaseFuzzyRulesClassifier(linguistic_variables=variables, ordinal_conditions=['temp', 'level'])
BaseFuzzyRulesClassifier(n_linguistic_variables=4, ordinal_conditions=[1, 2])   # by index

The other variables keep single terms (and any compound terms). A feature named explicitly must be able to have the conditions (3 or more terms, not categorical), otherwise a ValueError names it; an unknown name or an index out of range is an error too. A bare string, {'a': 1} or a number is rejected: pass a list. With True the variables that cannot have them are skipped quietly. An empty collection, False and None are off, and then the problem is built exactly as in 3.4.0.

Named compound terms#

Some variables are not ordinal in the way at least assumes. The terms of a clock hour are cyclic, so a useful condition is local_hour IS Night = the last term or the first one. compound_terms adds such conditions explicitly:

model = BaseFuzzyRulesClassifier(
    linguistic_variables=variables,
    ordinal_conditions=['temp'],                       # not the clock
    compound_terms={'local_hour': [('Night', ['Very High', 'Low']),   # wraps around midnight
                                   ('Daytime', ['Medium', 'High'])]},
    ...)
# IF local_hour IS Night AND temp IS at least Medium WITH DS ..., ACC ...

  • Shape. compound_terms is None or a mapping {feature: [(name, [term, ...]), ...]}. A feature is a name or an index (as in ordinal_conditions); each term is a term name or a term index. Every entry adds one condition x IS <name> to that variable, whose membership is the bounded sum of the listed terms. The terms need not be contiguous, so a union that wraps around is allowed. The set is kept sorted low to high ([last, first] and [first, last] are the same condition).

  • Validation (ValueError with the variable and the compound named): at least 2 distinct terms (a term listed twice is an error); not every term of the variable (it would be a don’t care); an unknown term name or an index out of range; no two compounds of a variable with the same set; no compound with the set of an ordinal condition the variable has (for example [T1, T2, T3] with at least T1); the name must be unique within its variable: it cannot be the name of a term, of another compound, or of an ordinal condition, and it cannot be empty, contain ;, line breaks, parentheses or the words AND, IS, WITH, THEN, nor start with at least / at most (the rule text must be readable back). Two keys for the same feature are an error too.

  • Together or alone. Compound terms work with or without ordinal_conditions on the same variable, with searched partitions (terms by name Low… or index) or linguistic_variables, with Type-1 and interval Type-2 sets, and also on categorical variables (any set of categories is a valid union). They are refused for general Type-2 sets and with candidate_rules. A variable needs 3 or more terms (2 of 2 terms is a don’t care). With ordinal conditions on, a contiguous run that touches an end repeats one of them (with 3 terms [T0, T1] is at most T1) and is refused; [T0, T2] is not and is accepted.

  • Membership. The bounded sum of the listed terms, as for at least / at most. A union that wraps around on a linear axis is two shoulders, one at each end: for [last, first] it is 1 at both ends of the domain, falls over the ramp of the first term, is 0 in the middle and rises over the ramp of the last term. On a strong partition it equals one minus the memberships of the other terms. Nothing here knows the axis is cyclic: a value just before and just after midnight are the two ends and both are covered.

  • Codes. After the single terms and the ordinal conditions: with n terms, the compounds are n_conditions - len(compound) ... n_conditions - 1, in the order given (condition_code('any_of', name)). They go through the same machinery (rows of compute_memberships, the gather table, the remap, canonical codes for duplicates, and the array, batch_size, population and PyTorch objectives; the latter declines with searched partitions).

A variable can be built with them directly: fuzzyVariable(name, sets, ordinal_conditions=False, compound_terms=[(name, [terms])...]) or fuzzy_sets.enable_conditions(variables, ordinal_conditions, compound_terms).

A variable can also offer the conditions on its own, for any code that reads rules: fuzzyVariable(name, sets, ordinal_conditions=True) or fuzzy_sets.enable_ordinal_conditions(variables).

Which conditions exist#

For a variable with n ordered terms t_0 ... t_(n-1): the n single terms, at least t_j for j = 1 ... n - 2 and at most t_j for j = 1 ... n - 2, that is 3n - 4 conditions (8 for 4 terms, 5 for 3). at least t_0 is always true (a don’t care) and at least t_(n-1) is t_(n-1), and likewise at most t_(n-1) and at most t_0, so they are not offered. General “between” intervals are not offered. No two conditions cover the same set of terms, so a set has exactly one code.

Membership#

The membership of a union is the bounded sum, the Lukasiewicz t-conorm:

\[\mu_{\text{at least } t_j}(x) = \min\Bigl(1, \sum_{k \ge j} \mu_{t_k}(x)\Bigr), \qquad \mu_{\text{at most } t_j}(x) = \min\Bigl(1, \sum_{k \le j} \mu_{t_k}(x)\Bigr)\]

  • Strong partitions. On a strong (Ruspini) partition, where the memberships of a point add up to 1, the sum never exceeds 1 and at least t_j is exactly 1 - (memberships of t_0 ... t_(j-1)): again a shouldered trapezoid, with the ramp of the term below t_j. The partitions built by utils.construct_partitions (trapezoidal) and construct_breakpoint_partition are strong. The terms of a partition found by the search (linguistic_variables=None) never add up to more than 1 but can add up to less (a point between two terms may belong to neither fully); the bounded sum is then just the sum, and at least t_j + at most t_(j-1) is below 1 there.

  • Partitions that are not strong. Gaussian terms overlap more than a partition of unity: their sum exceeds 1 and the bounded sum saturates at 1 there. Nothing else changes.

  • Interval Type-2. The lower and the upper bound are summed apart (and clipped to 1 each), which is the interval-arithmetic image of the bounded sum.

  • t-norm. A condition is one antecedent with a membership in [0, 1]; the rule still combines its antecedents with its t-norm (the product by default), so the union takes part exactly as a single term would. The bounded sum is the dual of the Lukasiewicz t-norm, not of the product, so a union is not the De Morgan dual of a product intersection of complements; it is chosen because it keeps the trapezoids and at least t_j + at most t_(j-1) = 1 on strong partitions.

  • A firing strength is still a degree of match, not a probability, contribution or risk.

How the search encodes it#

The term gene is widened, no gene is added. Without the option a term gene of slot i holds -1 (don’t care) or a value in 0 ... max_terms - 1, which the decoder maps onto the terms of whichever variable the slot’s variable gene selected (_fitness.remap_term). With the option the gene holds a condition code in 0 ... max_codes - 1, and the same proportional map is applied to the code count of the variable (FitRuleBase.n_term_codes; the number of terms when the option is off):

code

condition

0 ... n-1

IS t_code (single terms)

n ... 2n-3

IS at least t_j, j = code - n + 1

2n-2 ... 3n-5

IS at most t_j, j = code - (2n-2) + 1

RuleSimple.antecedents stores the same code (-1 for a don’t care), so a rule is identified, compared, hashed and de-duplicated by its codes as before; because a set of terms has one code, two rules whose conditions are the same sets are duplicates in the decoders (object, array, population and PyTorch), in RuleBase and in the mixture. Sampling, crossover and mutation work on the gene bounds, which are the wider ones, so they need no change. The fitness routes (array, streamed with batch_size, population and the PyTorch objective on fixed partitions) take the extra conditions as extra columns of the same membership table and agree with the object decoder bit for bit. With linguistic_variables=None the PyTorch objective, which builds trapezoids itself, declines and the CPU scores the fit.

fuzzyVariable.condition(code) gives (operator, term_index), condition_code(operator, term_index) the inverse, condition_terms(code) the terms covered and condition_names() the printed names. rules.rule_conditions(rule, antecedents) (also rule.conditions(antecedents)) returns Condition tuples (feature, variable, operator, term, term_index, terms, code, name) with operator one of 'is', 'at_least', 'at_most' and 'any_of'. For the first three term / term_index are the term the operator refers to, terms the terms covered and name is None; for 'any_of' term and term_index are None, terms lists the constituent term names (low to high) and name is the given name of the compound term. fuzzyVariable.condition(code) returns ('any_of', position in variable.compound) for it.

Search space and budget#

A variable of n terms offers 3n - 4 conditions instead of n: 5 instead of 3, 8 instead of 4. The gene length is unchanged but the space is larger, and uniform sampling draws a single term in only n / (3n - 4) of the cases (60 % for 3 terms, 50 % for 4). A search with the same n_gen and pop_size therefore has less of its budget on single terms; if the comparison with the default must be fair, give the option a larger budget or compare the best of several seeds (see benchmarks/benchmark_ordinal_conditions.py). Each condition still counts as one in the compactness penalty and in nAnts.

Printing, saving and loading#

print_rules writes IS at least Fair / IS at most Fair and IS <name> for a compound term (also print_mixture, the regression rules and vis_rules.rules_to_latex, which writes $\geq$ Fair / $\leq$ Fair). persistence.load_fuzzy_rules reads them back: a variable without ordinal conditions in the list you pass is replaced, in the returned rule base, by a copy that has them; the name of a compound term needs the variables to carry it, so load the variables first. save_fuzzy_variables writes the line Ordinal conditions after the header of a variable that has them and one line Compound;<name>;<term indexes> per compound term (variables without either are written exactly as before), and load_fuzzy_variables reads them. Pickles keep the option; a classifier, regressor or variable pickled before it existed loads with it off. pattern_stability rule keys name the condition (('x', 'at least Fair')) and consensus_rule_base rebuilds them.

Not covered#

FuzzyRulesClassifier (fuzzy association rules), RuleMineClassifier, RuleFineTuneClassifier, the FERL learners and the null distribution of the bootstrapped rule p-values (random rules over single terms) do not take the option.
